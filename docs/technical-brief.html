Card Fraud · Validation Summary

Unsupervised Fraud Triage on Qdrant

A per-cardholder anomaly score for identifying unfamiliar fraud patterns. On held-out synthetic data, it detected 57 of 60 fraud sequences and recorded a median decision latency under 14 ms at 11 million points. It is designed for analyst triage, not approval or decline.

What Was Tested

Each authorization becomes a 31-field vector covering transaction, location, merchant, and recent-history behavior. The score compares it only with the cardholder's prior activity.

score = d_event / d_local d_event mean distance from the event to its 10 nearest neighbors inside this cardholder's history d_local mean distance of those 10 neighbors from their own centroid

The denominator normalizes for normal variation. The evaluated threshold is 2.0, calibrated to roughly three months of history. Alerts retain their comparison events and score details.

Validation Design

Detection uses about 4,960 synthetic events per seed and 60 labeled fraud sequences across three patterns. The held-out seed uses different customers, traffic, and fraud placement from tuning. Latency uses a fresh collection, 100 warm-up events, and 1,000 single-threaded measurements; writes are excluded.

Scope And Next Steps

  • All detection and alert-volume results use synthetic cardholder and fraud data.
  • The evaluation measures single-query latency, not concurrent throughput or server hardware performance.
  • The alert threshold must be recalibrated when retention depth changes.
  • Tenant filtering provides query-level isolation; hard storage isolation needs a separate collection and encryption design.

Before deployment, validate alert rates on representative traffic, calibrate by retention depth, and benchmark concurrent traffic on production-like infrastructure.
